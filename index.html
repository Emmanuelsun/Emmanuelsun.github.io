AI lead conversion system for a renovation company

Simulated client. Built with n8n, Gemini, HubSpot and Mailpit, all free tools.

A renovation business loses jobs when homeowners wait a day for an answer. I built a system that scores each web-form inquiry, saves it to the CRM, alerts the owner to the good ones, answers the rest, and holds back any reply it is not sure about.

The three misses were borderline-area leads in Barrie, Guelph and Niagara Falls. The system flagged each one as uncertain and sent it to the owner instead of replying.

How a lead moves through it
  1. Form. The homeowner describes the project.
  2. Clean. Fields are tidied into one standard format.
  3. Classify. An AI model returns hot, warm, cold or spam, a score, a reason and a draft reply.
  4. Route. Fixed rules decide the path, so behaviour stays predictable.
  5. Save. The CRM gets the contact, once, with the AI’s findings.
  6. Act. Hot leads go to the owner. Others get a reply only if confidence and service-area checks pass.
  7. Watch. Failures trigger an email, so no lead disappears quietly.
What testing caught

A reply invented a company policy. A draft told a lead the business avoids small repairs, which nobody had said. I added a fact sheet of real services and a rule to say “I’m not sure” about anything unlisted.

The AI was fully confident about vague leads. A confidence threshold alone would have caught almost nothing, so I put plain rule-based checks behind it.

Free-tier limits broke my first test run. Most calls failed at five requests a minute. I throttled the test, moved to a stable model, and tested the same model I deploy.

About

My journey into AI automation began in growth marketing, where I worked on helping businesses attract customers and grow.

Along the way, I encountered a project where things weren’t working as they should. Processes were becoming difficult to manage, tasks weren’t flowing as smoothly as they needed to, and I began to see how operational inefficiencies could undermine even the best growth strategies.

That experience changed how I looked at technology.

I realized that helping a business grow isn’t just about getting more customers. It’s also about building the systems that enable the business to operate efficiently, manage its processes, and handle growth without unnecessary complexity. That’s what led me to AI automation and systems integration.

Today, I focus on helping businesses work smarter, operate more efficiently, and scale through technology.

My mission is simple: help businesses turn technology into practical systems that save time, reduce manual work, and support sustainable growth.

I do this by helping businesses:

  1. Automate repetitive tasks so teams can focus on higher-value work.
  2. Integrate business systems so their tools, data, and workflows work together seamlessly.
  3. Implement AI-powered solutions that streamline operations and improve productivity.
  4. Build smarter workflows that reduce inefficiencies and create room for growth.

My approach goes beyond simply introducing new tools. It starts with understanding how a business operates, identifying bottlenecks, and designing systems that solve real problems.

I believe powerful technology shouldn’t be reserved for large enterprises. Every business should have access to the tools, systems, and automation it needs to compete and grow.

When businesses spend less time dealing with manual processes and more time doing what they do best, that’s what drives me.
